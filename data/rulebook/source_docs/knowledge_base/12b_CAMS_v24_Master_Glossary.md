# 12b_CAMS_v24_Master_Glossary

CAMS AI KNOWLEDGE BASE v24.0  MASTER GLOSSARY AND TERMINOLOGY REFERENCE  AI TERMINOLOGY STANDARD  PURPOSE  This document defines CAMS terminology so another AI system interprets CAMS language  consistently.  The purpose is to prevent:  -   ambiguous reasoning,  -   incorrect terminology,  -   inconsistent application of audits,  -   confusion between visual effects and physiological effects.  Excluded:  -   client cases,  -   client images,  -   historical outcomes,  -   unavailable calibration examples.  ==================================================  1. CORE CAMS TERMS  ==================================================  CAMS
Definition:  A structured colour analysis methodology based on drape evidence, facial response, audits, and  physiological evaluation.  ---  Physiological Improvement  Definition:  A genuine improvement in the person's facial response created by a palette.  Includes:  -   healthier appearance,  -   improved continuity,  -   reduced unhealthy effects,  -   improved vitality.  Does not automatically mean:  -   stronger colour,  -   more contrast,  -   more dramatic appearance.  ---  Palette - Created Effect  Definition:
A visible change caused by colour interaction that may not represent improvement to the person.  Examples:  -   increased contrast,  -   increased saturation,  -   stronger colour impact,  -   deeper appearance.  ==================================================  2. FACIAL RESPONSE TERMS  ==================================================  Facial Information  Definition:  The preservation of important facial characteristics, natural structure, and visual identity.  Loss of facial information may appear as:  -   flattening,  -   suppression,  -   reduced vitality.  ---  Healthy Modelling  Definition:
Natural facial structure enhanced without creating unhealthy darkness.  Characteristics:  -   believable structure,  -   preserved anatomy,  -   improved integration.  ---  Unhealthy Shadow Burden  Definition:  Darkness or heaviness created by a palette that reduces facial harmony.  Examples:  -   under - eye heaviness,  -   harsh eye sockets,  -   dragged jaw appearance,  -   excessive cheek shadow.  ---  Facial Integration  Definition:  The degree to which the palette supports the entire face as a unified whole.
==================================================  3. SKIN RESPONSE TERMS  ==================================================  Believable Flesh Colour  Definition:  Skin colour that appears natural, healthy, and integrated.  ---  Authentic Pigmentation  Definition:  Natural - looking skin colour presence that belongs to the person.  Not:  -   reflected drape colour,  -   artificial redness,  -   colour pressure.  ---  Colour Pressure  Definition:  When a palette imposes visible colour intensity rather than supporting natural pigmentation.
---  Grey Veil  Definition:  A greyed or muted effect that may reduce vitality or facial presence.  ---  Suppression  Definition:  A reduction of facial information or natural colour activity that may appear smoother but is not  necessarily healthier.  ==================================================  4. VITALITY TERMS  ==================================================  Vitality  Definition:  The appearance of natural life, openness, animation, and healthy colour activity.  ---
Biological Animation  Definition:  A natural increase in liveliness, often shown through:  -   eyes,  -   facial openness,  -   person - first focus.  ---  Luminosity  Definition:  Natural facial openness and vitality.  Important:  Luminosity is not simply brightness.  ---  Grounding  Definition:  Support created by depth, richness, or stability when it benefits the person.  Grounding is not automatically:  -   heaviness,
-   darkness,  -   seriousness.  ==================================================  5. HARMONY TERMS  ==================================================  Harmony  Definition:  A balanced relationship where the palette supports the person without overpowering them.  ---  Person - First Harmony  Definition:  The principle that the individual remains the focus rather than the palette.  ---  Refinement  Definition:  An organized or polished appearance.  Important:
Refinement alone does not prove physiological improvement.  ---  Identity Preservation  Definition:  The ability of a palette to improve the person while maintaining their natural visual identity.  ==================================================  6. BOUNDARY TERMS  ==================================================  Boundary Decision  Definition:  A close comparison requiring additional audits and evidence.  ---  Bridge Palette  Definition:  A neighbouring palette direction that competes closely with another palette.  ---
True Palette Neutrality  Definition:  The rule that True palettes are candidates only and do not receive automatic preference.  ---  Runner - Up  Definition:  The closest neighbouring palette after final selection.  ==================================================  7. AUDIT TERMS  ==================================================  Independent Physiology Audit  Definition:  A check determining whether an advantage represents genuine facial improvement.  ---  Effect Source Separation  Definition:  Determining whether an effect comes from:
-   physiological improvement,  -   palette - created appearance,  -   suppression,  -   colour pressure.  ---  Natural Luminosity Audit  Definition:  Evaluation of genuine openness, sheen, eye vitality, and healthy colour activity.  ---  Warmth Source Audit  Definition:  Determining whether warmth comes from authentic pigmentation or added colour pressure.  ---  Shadow Reduction Audit  Definition:  Evaluation of whether unhealthy shadow decreases while facial structure remains.  ==================================================
8. DECISION TERMS  ==================================================  Cumulative Evidence  Definition:  The combined strength of multiple CAMS checks rather than reliance on one dramatic feature.  ---  Physiology Priority  Definition:  The principle that healthy facial response outranks secondary visual effects.  ---  Person - First Evaluation  Definition:  Evaluating what the palette does to the individual rather than how impressive the palette appears.  ==================================================  9. AI INTERPRETATION RULES  ==================================================  The AI must understand:
Brightness is not automatically vitality.  Warmth is not automatically health.  Depth is not automatically grounding.  Smoothness is not automatically improvement.  Contrast is not automatically harmony.  Refinement is not automatically superiority.  Colour impact is not automatically physiological benefit.  ==================================================  10.
